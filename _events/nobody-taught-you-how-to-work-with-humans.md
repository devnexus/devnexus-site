title: Nobody Taught You How to Work With Humans
description: "We spend years teaching engineers how to work with computers. How to
  debug them, structure them, make them faster, and keep them from doing wildly unexpected
  things.\r\n\r\nThen one day, the job quietly changes.\r\n\r\nSuddenly, your biggest
  dependency is people.\r\n\r\nAnd people have terrible documentation.\r\n\r\nThere’s
  no API. The error messages are vague. Production behavior is wildly inconsistent.
  And somehow, nobody thought this deserved a course.\r\n\r\nAs engineers become more
  senior, technical ability is still essential and being right isn’t enough anymore.
  Your impact increasingly depends on your ability to explain a technical problem
  to someone who isn’t technical, push back without becoming “the engineer who always
  says no,” disagree productively, communicate tradeoffs, influence decisions you
  don’t own, and figure out what someone is actually asking for.\r\n\r\nIn this session,
  we’ll explore the human skills that turn technical expertise into influence. We’ll
  look at how to translate, not dumb down, technical concepts for different audiences,
  how to challenge an idea while staying part of the solution, why curiosity is an
  underrated technical skill, and what happens when your impact starts being measured
  less by what you personally build and more by what you enable.\r\n\r\nBecause eventually,
  the hardest systems you’ll work on aren’t written in Python, C#, JavaScript, or
  PHP.\r\n\r\nThey’re made of people.\r\n"
